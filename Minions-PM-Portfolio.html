01 · The Problem

The right idea.
The right time?

South Africa had two persistent and connected problems — busy professionals with no time for errands, and high youth unemployment creating a pool of capable people who needed flexible income. Minions was designed to bridge them through an on-demand task platform.

"We were building what would later become the gig economy — in South Africa, in 2012, before smartphones were ubiquitous and before the category had a name. The problem was real. The timing and execution were the challenge."

02 · Honest Reflection

Right problem.
Wrong approach.

Minions failed not because the problem didn't exist — it absolutely did. It failed because we tried to solve it for everyone at once, without the infrastructure the market needed, ahead of its time.

No Beachhead
We tried to serve everyone — students, professionals, corporates, organisations.
Serving everyone means serving no one exceptionally. We needed one user, one problem, one solution. Had we targeted only law firms or busy mothers we'd have had a defensible market entry.
Market Timing
In 2012 most South Africans weren't yet transacting on mobile platforms.
The infrastructure for on-demand services — smartphones, digital payments, always-on connectivity — wasn't yet mainstream. The problem was real but the enabling technology wasn't ready at scale.
Unit Economics
Pricing between R70–R200 per errand without a clear cost structure.
A sustainable marketplace requires predictable take rates and margins. We hadn't modelled the unit economics before launching.
Founder Dependency
The business ended when co-founders graduated and entered corporate employment.
Without a succession plan or external team the business was entirely dependent on its founders being available. No operational scalability.

"Had we launched today — with smartphones in every pocket, a normalised gig economy, and digital payment infrastructure — the conditions would have been far more favourable. The insight wasn't wrong. The timing was."

03 · The Through-Line

Three products.
One lesson.

Across WealthSpaces, The Juice Bar, and Minions — the pattern is the same. A great product requires the right problem, a specific user, validated before building, and the right environment to grow in. Get any one of those wrong and even a great idea fails.

WealthSpaces
Skipped problem validation before building at scale.
The Juice Bar
Validated the problem. Iterated on data. Found product-market fit.
Minions
Right problem. No beachhead. Ahead of the infrastructure.
