A safety deposit box for journals.

Long-term journal keepers are stuck. They cannot safely keep their journals, cannot safely destroy them, and no tool resolves both problems. Heirloom is a secure preservation vault that gives people the confidence to let go.

01 · Problem

A paralysis with no solution

🔓
Privacy Risk
Physical journals can be read by anyone at any time — a family member, a partner, whoever finds them after death. The number one fear of journal keepers is someone reading without permission.
📦
Physical Burden
Journals accumulate. They move in boxes between countries. They take up space. For frequent movers and digital nomads, the weight is both physical and psychological.
🚫
No Trusted Exit
Existing tools — scanning apps, PDFs, photos — fail to preserve the visual and emotional authenticity of the original. People cannot bring themselves to destroy what isn't truly preserved.
Core Hypothesis
"Long-term journal keepers who fear privacy invasion or physical loss want a secure digital vault that preserves journals visually enough for them to destroy the physical originals."
"There isn't a great system currently for passing on digital journals."
Hannah Braime, digital journalling author
"One of the biggest fears people have as journal keepers is someone reading or doing something with their journal without their permission."
Lynda Monk, Director, International Association for Journal Writing

03 · Research

What the data revealed

🔍 6 Behaviours Identified
Store and hoard, re-read regularly, destroy with regret, scan inadequately, pass to family, and defer indefinitely. The majority of long-term journal keepers store and defer — not because they want to, but because no better option exists.
😰 5 Core Fears
Privacy invasion (#1), fire or flood loss, eBay or donation after death, physical burden, and legacy uncertainty. Privacy fear was consistent across every community observed — without exception.
🛠 DIY Workarounds All Fail
Phone photography, flatbed scanning, typing transcriptions, voice-to-text, fire and safe storage. Every workaround was described by real users as a "trudge" — too slow, too imperfect, or too emotionally unsatisfying to trust.
🎯 Strategic Decision
When survey respondents skewed toward art and bullet journallers — easier to find online — the decision was made to stay the course. Sample bias is not market signal. The harder-to-reach personal diary writer remains the core persona.
